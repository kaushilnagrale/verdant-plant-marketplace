## What it usually means

Yellowing of older, lower leaves first is very often overwatering - roots
sitting in wet soil can't take up oxygen properly and the plant responds
by shedding leaves. This is the single most common houseplant complaint
and the most common cause is too much water, not too little.

## What to check

Check the soil with a finger 2 inches down. If it's wet or the pot feels
heavy, hold off watering entirely until it dries out - do not water
"just in case". Make sure the pot has drainage and isn't sitting in a
saucer of standing water.

## When it's something else

Yellowing of just one or two leaves, especially older ones near the
base, can also be completely normal aging - plants naturally shed their
oldest leaves over time. Widespread, rapid yellowing across the whole
plant is the pattern worth acting on.

## When to escalate

If the stem near the soil is soft, dark, or mushy in addition to
yellowing leaves, this is likely root rot rather than simple
overwatering, and needs repotting into fresh, dry soil with any
blackened roots trimmed away - a task for a real nursery, not a home fix.
