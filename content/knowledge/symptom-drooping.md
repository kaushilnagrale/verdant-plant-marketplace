## What it usually means

Confusingly, drooping can mean either too much water or too little -
both stress the roots' ability to support the plant. The soil is the
deciding clue, not the leaves alone.

## What to check

If the soil is dry and the pot feels light, it's underwatering - water
thoroughly and the plant often recovers within hours. If the soil is
wet or waterlogged, it's more likely overwatering or root rot - stop
watering and check the roots.

## When it's something else

Sudden drooping right after a move (a new home, a repotting, a long
delivery journey) can be transplant/transit shock - this usually
resolves within a few days as the plant adjusts, without any change to
care needed.

## When to escalate

Drooping combined with a foul smell from the soil, or roots that are
brown and mushy rather than white/tan and firm, means root rot -
repotting into fresh soil with damaged roots trimmed is needed, not just
adjusting the watering schedule.
